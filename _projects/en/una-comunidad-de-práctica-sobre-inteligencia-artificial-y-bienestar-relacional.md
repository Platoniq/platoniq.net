The SoReDi Community of Practice (COP SoReDi) is an initiative launched by ESADE and the Platoniq Foundation that brings together young people aged between 16 and 25 to explore the social, ethical and democratic challenges of artificial intelligence and to play an active role in shaping their futures.

Through face-to-face meetings, online sessions, creative methodologies and collective deliberation processes, SoReDi offers a space in which young people are not merely users of technology, but can question it, imagine alternatives and play a part in defining the rules that govern how it works.

In a context characterised by the capture of attention, ‘connected loneliness’, technological dependence and the growing presence of artificial intelligence in our personal, educational and social relationships, the project poses a fundamental question: **which technologies do we want to be part of our lives, and under what conditions?**

##### What is the SoReDi Community of Practice?

The SoReDi CoP is a community for learning, experimentation and collective creation that places young people’s experiences at the heart of the debate on artificial intelligence.

Moving beyond digital literacy – understood as the acquisition of technical skills – the project focuses on the ability to understand, question and transform the technologies that shape our daily lives. It aims to explore how we can exercise our autonomy in the face of algorithmic systems and how we can participate in the decisions that shape our well-being, our relationships and our democratic coexistence.

The Community of Practice combines different methodologies and tools to help its participants:

* Identify the social and democratic conflicts associated with the use of artificial intelligence.
* Share experiences and build collective knowledge drawing on a diversity of perspectives.
* Explore dilemmas relating to mental health, autonomy, care and algorithmic justice.
* Formulate citizens’ mandates to guide the development of socially responsible technologies.
* Envision and prototype technological alternatives that meet real needs.
* Participate in defining the limits and conditions of use for artificial intelligence.

**It is not just about learning how to use artificial intelligence, but about developing our collective capacity to decide what we want it to do and what we do not want it to do.**

##### What has the experience been like so far?

At Platoniq, we have designed a methodological framework that combines elements of deliberative democracy, legislative theatre, speculative design, creative thinking and collaborative prototyping.

The process is structured around a series of bootcamps and online meetings that enable participants to progress step by step from identifying conflicts and needs to formulating proposals and designing technological solutions.

During the first bootcamp, participants explored different situations relating to ‘connected loneliness’, attention capture, new forms of community, AI-mediated self-exploitation and radicalisation in digital environments.

Through role-play scenarios and group dilemmas, the young people were able to discuss the contradictions that underpin their relationship with technology: tools that facilitate connection but can also lead to isolation; systems that offer support but can create dependency; and apps that boost our productivity but can also intensify the pressure to constantly optimise our lives.

These discussions made it possible to identify shared needs and formulate the first citizen mandates relating to digital wellbeing, personal autonomy and algorithmic justice.

##### From legislative theatre to technology design

One of Platoniq’s key methodological contributions to SoReDi is the adaptation of legislative theatre to the field of technology governance, through a methodology we call *Legislative Tech Theatre*.

Inspired by Augusto Boal’s Theatre of the Oppressed, this methodology uses the performing arts to highlight the power struggles that arise in our relationship with technology and to transform participants’ contributions into concrete proposals.

The scenes depict everyday situations in which artificial intelligence plays a significant role. Participants can intervene, take on the roles of the characters, question their decisions and explore other ways of engaging with technology.

The methodological process explores five areas in depth:

1. **Explore (experiences):** identify situations in which technologies give rise to conflicts, inequalities or new forms of dependency.
2. **Deliberate (dilemmas):** compare perspectives and recognise the conflicting needs, interests and values.
3. **Legislate (mandates):** transform collective reflections into conditions, principles and limits that should guide technological development.
4. **Envision (alternatives):** design technologies that respond to the mandates formulated by the participants.
5. **Experiment (prototypes):** test the proposals, identify their contradictions and collectively review their functionalities.

In this way, theatre ceases to be merely a tool for raising awareness and becomes a space for democratic experimentation and technological design.

**What would happen if the people who use a technology could also participate in writing its rules?**

##### From mandates to ideas: three artificial intelligence prototypes

At the second COP SoReDi bootcamp, we took it a step further: transforming the citizens’ mandates developed during previous sessions into concrete proposals for artificial intelligence.

Participants worked on three lines of experimentation: artificial intelligence geared towards learning and study; a tool for emotional support; and artificial intelligence designed to facilitate the formation of one’s own opinions and critical thinking.

The challenge was not simply to imagine innovative applications, but to design tools capable of incorporating the values, needs and limits defined collectively.

To this end, the teams worked with user stories, role-plays, stakeholder maps, fanzines and prototyping tools. Each proposal had to answer a series of fundamental questions:

* What needs does this AI address?
* Which people might benefit from it, and who might be excluded?
* What functions should it incorporate?
* What actions should it never carry out?
* Which decisions must remain in people’s hands?
* How can we ensure that it respects the community’s guidelines?

The proposals were subjected to further role-play scenarios, which made it possible to explore their potential contradictions and review the rules that would govern their operation.

The result of this process is three concepts for artificial intelligence prototypes that stem from collective deliberation and place people’s wellbeing, autonomy and critical thinking at the heart of their design.

##### A community experimenting with digital democracy

The digital dimension of SoReDi is not limited to the project’s subject matter. It also forms part of its participatory methodology.

The open-source citizen participation platform Decidim enables the process to be documented, materials to be shared, proposals to be collected and the conversations generated during face-to-face meetings to be continued.

This space contributes to the traceability of collective decisions, making it easier for participants to retrieve the mandates formulated, review the proposals and understand how their contributions are incorporated into the development of the prototypes.

Furthermore, through its own newsletter, the Community of Practice shares resources, initiatives, news and opportunities relating to artificial intelligence, digital rights and democratic innovation.

The aim is to create a space for participation that does not end when a workshop finishes, but rather enables a community to remain active, learning, debating and experimenting together.

##### What do participants take away from the programme?

The SoReDi Community of Practice aims to enhance young people’s ability to engage critically and creatively in debates on artificial intelligence.

Throughout the process, participants have the opportunity to explore their own digital experiences, compare them with those of others, recognise power struggles and acquire tools to turn their concerns into concrete proposals.

The project offers a learning experience that combines digital skills, critical thinking, creativity, democratic deliberation and advocacy skills.

It also allows us to experiment with other forms of technological innovation, where the starting point is not an existing technology or a market opportunity, but a collectively identified social need.

**Before asking ourselves what we can do with artificial intelligence, we must ask ourselves what is worth doing with it.**

##### Towards democratically designed artificial intelligence

The SoReDi Community of Practice will continue to develop until 2028, combining new meetings, deliberative processes and technological experimentation.

Among its upcoming challenges are making progress on the development of the prototypes, bringing new voices into the process, and establishing partnerships with communities of developers, technology professionals, researchers and organisations committed to digital rights.

At Platoniq, we see this project as an opportunity to explore new forms of citizen participation in the design and governance of artificial intelligence. A space where democratic methodologies, the performing arts and open technologies can come together to build alternatives to the dominant models of innovation.

SoReDi is more than just a digital literacy programme: it is a laboratory for democratic experimentation where younger generations can claim their role in shaping technological futures.

**Because the future of artificial intelligence should not depend solely on those who have the capacity to develop it, but also on those who will have to live with it.**
